Tournament Overview

Tournament Format: Pool Play Round Robin Style, with top teams advancing to the elimination bracket.

Match Rules: Games to 11, Win by 2. In tournament play, teams switch sides of the court after a team reaches 6 points to keep it fair due to elements like wind and sun.

Official Pickleball Rules (2026)

  • Scoring: Only the serving side scores. Games are played to 11 points and must be won by 2.
  • Two-Bounce Rule: The serve must bounce before the receiver returns it, and the return must bounce before the serving side plays it.
  • Serve: The serve is made diagonally, clears the non-volley zone, and must be made underhand with the contact below the waist.
  • Non-Volley Zone: A player may not volley while touching the non-volley zone or its line, including when momentum carries the player into it.
  • Line Calls: Players call the lines on their side of the court. When uncertain, the call is made in favor of the opponent.

Options & Guidelines for 80-Team (160-Player) Tournaments

For a 80-team (160-player) pickleball tournament, the best approach is to divide the field into 14 to 16 pools of 5-6 teams each, with 2-3 top teams from each pool advancing to a single-elimination bracket. This structure optimizes court usage and guarantees at least 4-5 matches, providing a high-quality experience for participants.

Recommended Structure (80 Teams)
  • Pool Setup: 16 pools of 5 teams (allows for efficient 4-5 matches per team) or 12 pools of 6–7 teams.
  • Advancement: Take the top 2-3 teams from each pool to form a 16 or 32-team single-elimination bracket (depending on court availability and time).
  • Game Format: Single games to 15 (win by 1 or 2) are ideal for large round-robins.
Key Considerations
  • Time Management: Pool play with 80 teams takes time. Ensure you have enough courts—aim for at least 16-20 courts to avoid long waits.
  • Tie-Breakers: With many pools, use point differential or head-to-head results to determine tie-breakers for bracket seeding.
  • "Best of" Scenario: To maximize "bang for your buck," allow 4 teams to advance from each pool to a larger bracket if you have time, or a shorter, higher-stakes bracket if time is tight.
